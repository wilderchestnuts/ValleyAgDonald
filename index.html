How Degree Days Work

Filbertworm development only occurs above 50°F. Each day contributes:
DD = (Tmax + Tmin) / 2 − 50 (floored at 0)

Accumulated from April 1, degree days predict when moths fly, eggs hatch, and larvae enter nuts — enabling precision spray timing.

About This Service

  • Traps are placed and checked weekly by Valley Agronomics scouts
  • Delta traps baited with C. latiferreana pheromone lure
  • Data is updated weekly throughout the flight season (May–August)
  • Spray recommendations are sent via text message each week
  • Contact your Valley Ag adviser with any questions

About the Degree Day Model

  • Base temperature 50°F, biofix April 1
  • Heat accumulation predicts filbertworm flight timing
  • 610 DD — first adult moths typically appear
  • 1,023 DD — peak egg hatch, larvae most vulnerable
  • 1,188 DD — peak adult emergence
  • Model data from OSU/USPest.org
